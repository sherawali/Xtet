The celebration of "workaholism" has long been a foundational pillar of corporate culture. Employees who routinely sacrifice their sleep, personal relationships and leisure time are consistently rewarded with promotions and public praise. This glorification of constant busyness treats human labour as a machine that can run indefinitely without maintenance. However, this hyper-productive ethos masks a deeper structural crisis: severe mental burnout and a collapse of employee well-being. When a workspace transforms into a pressure cooker of endless deadlines, creativity is ironically the very first casualty. True innovation requires cognitive breathing room — periods of stillness where the brain can connect unrelated ideas.
